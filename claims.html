Claims register

This is the detailed evidence trail behind index.md §6. The index table is a quick reference; this file is authoritative when the two differ. Every entry records what was claimed, when, by whom, what changed, and what would settle it.

The register exists because the corpus spans 2024-08-04 to 2026-10-22 and Tyler repeatedly revised his own positions. Quoting a two-year-old video as if it were current is the most common way to misuse this database.

How to read an entry

Each claim has:

  • Status — one of the categories below.
  • Established — what the record actually supports, separated from inference.
  • Position history — dated changes, with the confidence label used in the source summary.
  • Assessment — why the current position is where it is.
  • What would settle it — the artifact or statement that would end the debate.
  • Sources — the summaries to read, plus event counts where useful.

Status vocabulary

Status Meaning
Corrected An earlier position was wrong or misattributed. Use the current one.
Debunked A positive claim was tested and rejected. Do not repeat it.
Superseded Events moved past a prediction or expectation. Not a lie at the time.
Unresolved Competing positions remain; neither is established.
Unconfirmed A single-source identification or inference. Could be right; not proven.
Nuanced Roughly right, but the shorthand is misleading.
Updated Later data expanded or completed it without contradiction.

Evidence tiers used in the position histories

Tier Meaning
Primary A named, checkable artifact: patch contents, official post, published interview, store listing, shipped build.
Datamine A claim about build contents, usually checkable in principle but not re-checked here.
Secondhand Named journalist/outlet reporting, or an on-record quote reproduced by Tyler.
Unnamed source Tyler’s contacts, described but not identified.
Inference Tyler’s reasoning from other evidence.

Fact in a summary means a named artifact supports the claim; it does not mean this project independently verified the artifact. Verified in events.tsv means the artifact is directly checkable. The two labels overlap but are not identical.

C01. Project White Sands is HLX

Status: Corrected. “Project White Sands” is a Deadlock voice-casting codename. It is not HLX and should not be cited as HLX evidence.

Established. On 2024-08-04 a voice actor’s portfolio/resumé listing for “Project White Sands” surfaced. Tyler discussed it in HLX Files #0 and treated it as a Valve casting cover name connected to HLX. In Every Last Detail Ep1 (2026-01-29) he corrected the record: White Sands was a code name used for Deadlock auditions, and the voice actors themselves confirmed it. Miro #0849 and #0850 record the same conclusion.

Position history

Date Source Position Tier
2024-08-04 HLX Files #0 White Sands is a Valve casting codename; Tyler connects it to HLX. Primary listing + inference
2024-08-04 Community reaction HLX = Half-Life 3 and possibly imminent, based on the listing. Inference
2026-01-29 Every Last Detail Ep1 White Sands is Deadlock; actors confirmed. Primary (actor statements)
2026-01-29 Miro #0849/#0850 Same correction; Deadlock also used “Citadel” and “Project8.” Board note

Assessment. The correction is well supported by multiple actor statements and is one of the few places where Miro and the latest video agree outright. The original portfolio page was not archived in a privacy-safe form, so the initial discovery still lacks a primary snapshot. That is a documentation gap, not a reason to doubt the correction.

What would settle it. A privacy-safe archived copy of the 2024 casting listing, or an on-record actor statement naming Deadlock and White Sands together. Neither is required to stop using White Sands as HLX evidence.

C02. HLX is only an engine feature or save format

Status: Superseded. The “HLX is just a save format / engine flag” argument was a real early hypothesis. Tyler rejected it in 2024 and the subsequent asset record made it untenable. The stronger claim — that HLX is a full Half-Life game — remains his inference, not an official statement.

Established. The codename appears in public Source 2 builds from 2020-10-09 onward. By 2024 the associated material included HEV suit strings, off-world references, creature names, vehicle simulation, and Hammer tooling. A save-format or single engine flag would not accumulate that surface area. Tyler’s own framing in #0 was “a fully-fledged non-VR Half-Life game,” labeled Speculation.

Position history

Date Source Position Tier
2021–2024 Community HLX might be an engine feature or save-file format. Inference

2024-08-25 → 2026 Later videos Treats HLX as a game project with content, tools and staff. Accumulated datamine

Assessment. The negative claim (“not merely a save format”) is well supported by the breadth of HLX-labeled content. The positive claim (“a full game”) is still an inference from that content. The distinction matters: a project can have extensive engine work without being the game people expect.

What would settle it. A shipping build, store page, or Valve statement. Until then, describe HLX as “a Half-Life-shaped project,” not “confirmed Half-Life 3.”

C03. HLX is open world

Status: Debunked. HLX is better described as “open linear”: wide, open-ended levels with procedural activities, bounded by Source 2’s technical limits. It is not a seamless open world.

Established. The open-world claim did not originate with Tyler. In HLX Files #2 (2024-09-12) he explicitly corrects a public claim attributed to Gabe Follower, saying Half-Life 3 is not open world or pseudo-open world, but a linear game with much wider levels. In Ep1 (2026-01-29) he supplies the technical argument: Hammer’s walk limit remains 16,384 units, and Source 2’s baked-lightmap architecture makes a seamless open world impractical. He compares the intended scale to Doom 2016’s Foundry or open-ended Uncharted 4 levels.

Position history

Date Source Position Tier

2024-09-12 HLX Files #2 Not open world; wide-linear levels with high replayability. Inference
2024-11-22 HLX Files #5 “Open-linear” structure with immersive-sim elements. Inference

Assessment. This entry corrects a mistake in the earlier index table, which implied the open-world reading persisted into 2025. Tyler rejected it within five weeks of the first leaks. The technical argument is stronger than the terminology: the 16,384-unit limit and lightmap dependency are checkable engine facts, while “open linear” is a design comparison.

What would settle it. Map dimensions and streaming data from an HLX build, or gameplay footage showing contiguous traversal. Neither exists publicly.

C05. HLX is a VR game

Status: Debunked by an official statement. Valve’s Steam Frame team said on 2025-11-12 that it built no VR game or demos for the headset, and Tyler reports that this covers HLX. The design evidence also points away from VR.

Established. Tyler said HLX was non-VR as early as HLX Files #0 (2024-08-04), and repeated it in #1 (2024-08-25), while treating Valve’s Deckard headset as a separate hardware project. C08. HLX’s vehicle entities were deleted

Status: Nuanced. The shorthand “vehicles were cut” is wrong. An early vehicle schema exposed in Dota 2 in June 2022 was removed after dataminers found it; the vehicle system itself continued to evolve.

Established. Ep2 (2026-03-01) describes a vehicle schema and related entities added on 2022-06-09 and deleted in subsequent updates.
