Overview

Tyler argues that HLX (Half-Life 3) remains in active production despite Valve’s attention on Deadlock, Dota 2, and Counter-Strike 2, and that each major content drop for those games carries new HLX-related hints. The central new item is a shipment of roughly 21 .h build files from Valve’s cancelled voxel project “Arty” inside a Dota 2 update, which he treats as the most detailed look yet at that game and as evidence that Arty systems are being ported into HLX. He also catalogues NPC AI, level-streaming, and physics changes datamined from Dota 2, including a new Point Orient entity and a shotgun sound added to the Source 2 core. His tone is highly confident about the existence of the files, while the conclusions about how they are used in HLX are explicitly framed as inference.

He acknowledges employee time has gone to polishing Deadlock, finishing promised Dota 2 updates, and improving CS2, but maintains HLX/Half-Life 3 is “absolutely still in production.”

Shared environments as pseudo level streaming

  • Description: Tyler describes “shared environments” as the space visible behind the player during a level transition, where parts of the previous level remain visible. He says it is a trick that previously involved copy-pasting map geometry and loading it twice, but now geometry is loaded once and the rest of the level is streamed, producing a seamless transition without duplicate loading.
  • Confidence: Mixed — the existence of shared-environment keys is presented as datamined; the description of the streaming implementation is Tyler’s explanation.
  • Category: Engine feature
  • Source: Datamine of Source 2 content; Tyler’s technical explanation.

Background on the cancelled Arty project

  • Description: Tyler explains that Arty was a cancelled Valve game developed after the cancellation of Left 4 Dead 3, while Valve waited for Source 2 to become feature-complete. He describes it as an entirely voxel-based engine with player destruction, akin to a basic immersive sim with large procedural destruction potential, and says the only prior evidence came from The Final Hours of Half-Life: Alyx — story treatments, screenshots, and general developer descriptions.
  • Confidence: Mixed — the Final Hours material is a named prior source, while the engine and gameplay characterization is Tyler’s synthesis of the newly shipped files.
  • Category: Development state
  • Source: The Final Hours of Half-Life: Alyx plus the newly datamined Arty .h files.
  • Earlier mentions: References prior community knowledge of Arty from The Final Hours of Half-Life: Alyx; the new files greatly expand what is known.
  • Verification: needs follow-up — would require cross-checking the .h file contents against the Final Hours descriptions.

  • Description: Tyler says the community previously speculated that voxel work in Source 2 was for Neon Prime, but concludes “it had to do with HLX” and that Half-Life 3 “is going to have voxel based destruction in some form.” He argues Valve would reuse Arty’s existing voxel technology rather than rewrite it.
  • Confidence: Speculation — stated forcefully (“why not take the things that they had already worked on”), but based on inference from reused technology rather than a direct HLX artifact.
  • Category: Gameplay
  • Source: Tyler’s inference from Source 2 voxel code and the Arty file leak.

Physical fidelity, immersive-sim elements, and open-linear structure

  • Description: Tyler says Valve is pushing “physical fidelity” rather than graphical fidelity, simulating many systems independently so they can interact, and describes the result as resembling a next-generation immersive sim. He says Half-Life 3 is not an immersive sim at its core but will have more immersive-sim elements than prior Half-Life games, citing the Quest NPC system, procedural generation, and side-level designs. He rejects the open-world reading and describes the structure as “open linear,” closer to Uncharted 4 than Fallout.
  • Confidence: Speculation — Tyler’s interpretation of design direction, with explicit hedging (“I don’t think,” “I think”); the Quest NPC and procedural-generation systems are referenced but not demonstrated.
  • Category: Gameplay
  • Source: Tyler’s analysis of datamined systems and prior HLX reporting.
