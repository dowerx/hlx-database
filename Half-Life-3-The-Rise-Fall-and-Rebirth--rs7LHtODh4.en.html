Overview

This is a chronological retrospective of Half-Life 3 from the 1999 registration of halflife3.com through Episode Three’s cancellation, the 2015 Source 2 project, the 2021 HLX prototype, and 2024 Deadlock datamines. Tyler’s thesis is that “Half-Life 3” has named several different projects: a contractual placeholder after the Prospero games were dropped, the Episode Three trilogy, a 2015 Source 2 build, and the later HLX prototype. Most of the runtime recaps public interviews, magazine reports, leaks, and datamines rather than presenting new documents. Tyler is confident on the documented record and flags his main inferences: the “Old Man” G-Man sculpt, the Sephilon correspondence, HLX’s start date, and the Deadlock asset attribution.

halflife3.com registration and the Sierra/Prospero contract

  • Description: On June 28, 1999, Valve registered halflife3.com and halflife2.com (transcribed “Halflife22.com”). Tyler ties this to Valve’s Sierra publishing contract, public since the 2003 net-café lawsuit, which originally committed Valve to Half-Life plus Prospero 1 and Prospero 2. With Prospero abandoned, he argues the obligation was likely converted into three Half-Life games. He notes Counter-Strike was retailed as “Half-Life: Counter-Strike” to satisfy the contract despite having nothing to do with Half-Life.
  • Confidence: Mixed — the registration and the public contract are documentary; the claim that the contract was renegotiated into three Half-Life titles is Tyler’s inference (“likely changed over”).
  • Category: Business/legal
  • Source: Domain registration records and the 2003 net-café lawsuit contract, as described by Tyler.
  • Earlier mentions: None identified in this video.
  • Verification: verified for the domain and contract; needs follow-up for the contract-conversion claim.

New or changed in this video

  • The video is mostly a recap: it synthesizes roughly 25 years of public statements, magazine coverage, leaks, and datamines, and debuts no new primary document.
  • Tyler states explicitly that Episode Three had already been shelved by mid-2009, using the G4 and Steamcast interviews as the pivot.
  • He disputes Valve’s 2010 characterization of the weaponizer and identifies it as an Episode Three weapon visible in Noclip’s documentary footage.
  • He says Episode Three snow textures were long known but only recently recognized as Episode Three assets.
  • He links the 43-member “Half-Life 3” Jira group from June 2013 to the procedural-generation project described in The Final Hours of Half-Life: Alyx.
  • He dates the HLX prototype’s start to the March 21, 2021 Dota 2 update, almost a year after Alyx.
  • He presents Half-Life: Alyx’s spy grenades and zip lines as corroboration of the 2015 HL3TXT leak.
  • He lists 2024 Deadlock datamine entities as renewing Half-Life 3 speculation but does not claim they are confirmed HL3 content.
  • The closing minutes reuse a clip from Tyler’s earlier Episode Three video, which predicted the episodic model would become a larger Half-Life 3.
