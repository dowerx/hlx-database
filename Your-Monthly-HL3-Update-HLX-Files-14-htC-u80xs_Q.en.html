Overview

Tyler’s monthly HLX roundup covers a batch of new entity and FGD strings from the data mines and a Perforce commit graph he says shows August 2025 as Valve’s busiest build month in roughly a decade. The datamine material centers on a new HLX-specific liquid simulation with oil and water types, an ability-positioning entity, a destructible-part event tied to enemy AI, vehicle entity changes including a removed jeep, a new-versus-legacy save-system toggle, and cleanup of the AI sound entity into an “AI volumetric event” with new reaction inputs. He also notes load-time logging added in a Counter-Strike 2 update, which he reads as Valve optimizing level load times. He is explicit that he does not know the release date and that “within a year” is his own expectation, not a Valve statement. The episode also covers a Half-Life: Alyx soundtrack vinyl release and the Air Exchange Revised mod.

He says he was asked when Half-Life 3 is coming out 129 separate times, including by Dave Ashri (as transcribed), and at least once every 15 minutes. He jokes that he began telling people it had been announced the day before and they had missed it on a plane.

August 2025 was Valve’s busiest build month in about a decade

  • Description: Tyler presents a graph where each point is the number of changes pushed to Valve’s internal Perforce within the prior 30 days, based on publicly visible build/commit numbers. He says August 2025 was Valve’s busiest month in the last 10 years in terms of pushing changes to builds, and that the most Perforce commits in a single 7-day period since at least 2016 also occurred in August 2025. He stresses the data covers all internal Valve projects, not just HLX, and names Deadlock, Dota 2, Counter-Strike 2, HLX, Team Fortress, and “some other fun stuff” as active work.
  • Confidence: Mixed — the individual build-number increments are publicly observable, but the “busiest in 10 years” ranking depends on Tyler’s own tracked dataset going back to 2016, and the link between commit volume and HLX progress is his interpretation.
  • Category: Development state
  • Source: Tyler’s analysis of publicly published Valve build/commit numbers charted over time.
