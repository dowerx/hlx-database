Half-Life 3 / HLX — Development & Features Database

A structured record of what Tyler McVicker’s HLX coverage claims about Valve’s unannounced Half-Life project, how those claims changed, and how they compare with the Miro research board.

Coverage: 31 videos, 2024-08-04 → 2026-10-22, plus the Miro export. Extracted claims: 610 events across 31 per-video summaries. Official release date: none. Valve has never announced Half-Life 3, HLX, or a launch window. Latest state: as of HLX Files #20 (2026-10-22), Tyler expects no HLX announcement in 2026.

The single most important caveat: “HLX” is a datamined project codename. Tyler identifies HLX as Half-Life 3, and the accumulated evidence is strongly Half-Life-shaped, but Valve has never publicly confirmed that HLX is Half-Life 3. Statements below distinguish Tyler’s identification from official confirmation.

1. Read me first

This database is organized around four different kinds of statement, which are easy to blur together:

Layer Meaning Example
Primary artifact / official statement A named, checkable thing: a patch string, a published interview, a store listing, a talk recording. The Artifact Foundry box_creature_model_hlx.vanmgrph string; the Steam Frame team saying HLX is not VR.
Tyler’s inference Tyler’s reading of artifacts, sources, or Valve’s behaviour. Often plausible, sometimes hedged. “HLX is Half-Life 3”; “open-linear, not open world”; “announcement by end of 2025.”
Secondhand / unnamed sourcing Playtest reports, “whole floor” claims, trailer rumours, insider release chatter. Gabe Follower’s external-playtest report; the “entire floor” claim in #19.
Debunked / superseded A claim the corpus later corrects or abandons. White Sands = HLX; voxel destruction; open world; 2025 release.

Confidence labels in the per-video summaries:

  • Fact — directly supported by a named primary artifact, official statement, datamine, store page, court document, etc.
  • Speculation — Tyler’s inference, expectation, or opinion.
  • Mixed — some parts evidenced, others inferred; the summary explains which.
  • Unclear — the basis is not made clear.

“Fact” does not mean “independently verified by this project.” Many “Fact” entries are checkable artifacts that were not re-checked here. The verification field in each summary is the audit trail: verified, needs follow-up, unverified, or a partial combination.

The shortest possible history

  1. 1999–2017: several different “Half-Life 3” projects, including the Episode Three trilogy, a 2013–2015 attempt, and Epistle 3. None is HLX.
  2. 2019–2020: Half-Life: Alyx retcons Eli’s death; Valve finishes Alyx; HLX takes shape and appears in public Source 2 builds by September–October 2020.
  3. 2020–2024: HLX grows through shared Source 2 engine work; the codename leaks; dataminers find Half-Life-specific VData, NPC, vehicle, and tooling strings.
  4. 2024–2025: Tyler argues HLX is a full game and enters late production; external playtests are reported; a 2025 announcement is expected and then missed.
  5. 2026: The project is still in development after a semi-reboot. Valve says HLX is not VR. Tyler’s latest prediction is no announcement in 2026.

This is the documented background, not the HLX datamine story. The important point is that “Half-Life 3” has named several different projects.

5. Thematic dossiers

These are cross-video syntheses. Each claim inherits the confidence of its underlying events; “expected to appear in HLX” is Tyler’s framing unless stated otherwise.

5.1 Project identity and production history

What is solid: HLX is a real internal codename present in public Source 2 builds since at least October 2020. It was in production before the September 22, 2020 Sandbox builds. The codename became public knowledge on March 25, 2021. A 2020–2023 former-employee résumé describes work on an unannounced title. Valve hired heavily from other studios in 2021–2023 and acqui-hired most of Hopoo Games in 2024.

What is Tyler’s identification: that HLX is Half-Life 3. The supporting pattern is Half-Life-specific: HEV-suit sound references, “offworld,” Xen fauna, feral manhacks, Half-Life-style weapon buckets, a gravity gun, an HEV-suited protagonist, and Half-Life Hammer tooling. None of that is a Valve announcement.

What is secondhand: external playtesting (Gabe Follower, December 2024), the “entire floor” claim (2026), the trailer-work rumour (2025), and the semi-reboot/chapter-rebuild story. Tyler hedges each.

What changed: the project is not the 2013–2015 “posthuman” Half-Life 3 or the 2015 Source 2 build. The Final Hours confirms a 2015 Half-Life 3, but Tyler treats HLX as a later project that took shape around the Alyx ending. He also corrects the idea that the 2018 small team in The Final Hours was HLX: that line runs Citadel → Neon Prime → Shadow Line → Deadlock. Separately, his own 2024 voxel-destruction claim (#2/#5) was walked back in #9 and debunked in Ep1.

Current production read: Tyler’s latest position is intensive development/polish, not pre-production. The 2025 announcement window was missed. #19 says the project survived a semi-reboot and that his earlier chapter-rebuild explanation no longer holds; he does not state the replacement claim.

5.2 Engine, rendering, audio and tools

Shared-engine method. Valve does not fork Source 2 per title. Features developed for Dota 2, CS2, Deadlock or HLX appear across the others, so patch-diffing one game can expose another project’s needs. This is the methodological premise of the whole series; Tyler states it as fact, and the cross-game strings support it.

VData. A designer-driven data system that lets gameplay values and content definitions change without recompiling code. HLX’s weapons, NPCs, crates, crate items, grenades, vehicles, liquids, precipitation and map entities are all described as VData-driven. VData existed by 2020-08-12 and first appeared publicly on 2020-09-04. Its presence is verified; the claim that every listed HLX category uses it is Tyler’s reading of the schemas.

Animgraph 2. The replacement animation system: .vnmgraph graphs, .vnmclip clips, .vnmskel skeletons, .vnmvar variations (later deprecated), bone LOD, additive clips, legacy animevents, and networked efficiency. Deadlock moved its heroes to AG2; CS2 shipped AG2 for viewmodels on 2025-07-29 and, per Miro #0857, uses AG2 for everything from 2026-04-01 with AG1 removed. Ep6 found early HLX AG2 use in nav-link transitions, but HLX-specific AG2 deployment remains inferred.

Rendering. TAA and upscaling (initially FSR3, later more than FSR), ray-tracing support reported in Source 2 in late 2025, mesh shaders and meshlets, distance fields for AO/shadows/reflections, hair-strand simulation, subpixel anti-aliasing marked HLX-exclusive, GPU-driven particles, volumetric smoke with HLX refraction, dynamic shadow/RTP proxy work, and “only render in effects water pass” flags. The features are observable; which ones ship in HLX is not.

Audio. Procedural runtime sound generation, sound containers/stacks, a MIDI beat system, GPU-driven Hammer audio, precipitation audio operators that respond to surface and shelter, Speech Graphics lip-sync middleware, vehicle wind/RPM synthesis, and tape-player-style containers. Tyler reads plain-language tooltips and per-category asset icons as public-facing polish.

5.3 Simulation, physics and destruction

Fire and heat. Surface attributes carry flash point and heat conductivity. NPCs ignite after enough exposure above their flash point (ai_time_to_ignite, default 0.3). Held objects have ignition temperature and hold-time rules. Fire interacts with oil and other liquid properties. This is a generalized material-reaction system, not a scripted set piece.

Water and liquids. Source 2 adds buoyancy and drowning, liquid spills that spawn puddles, oil and water liquid types, waterfall helpers, a second water material, and an “only render in effects water pass” flag. HLX-specific liquid-property containers date to 2025-08-18.

Wind and weather. Fans with three particle types, wind affecting fog/grass/trees/hair/cloth, precipitation moved to VData, precipitation audio that changes with surface and shelter, and asynchronous particle wind simulation.

Physics and destruction. Multithreaded physics, deformable geometry, ragdoll mass and buoyancy scales, zero-gravity and no-collision attributes, destructible hit groups, destruction-death behavior, radius damage for destructible parts, breakable props that spawn sub-props and contained items, and NPC body-part destruction. Tyler compares the body-part system to Doom Eternal weak points but marks the combat application as inference.

Gravity shifting. Player and NPC traversal of gravity-bent spaces: walking on ceilings, warping across surfaces, orbiting. Compared to Super Mario Galaxy and Prey (2006). The tech is datamined; combat integration is inferred.

Vehicles. A modular, VData-driven, physics-based system: multiple passengers, passenger weapons, speed-dependent FOV, tire friction, suspension, wheel drag, surface-dependent handling, rollover/flip recovery, entry/exit rules, hit groups for occupants, and controller assists. Miro adds passenger enter/exit animations, passenger roles, and destructible car parts (#0410–#0544). The first schema leaked in a 2022-06-09 Dota 2 update and was removed in later updates; Miro #1487 says it changed again in November 2022 and March 2023 with CWheeledPhysicsVehicleVData, and Miro #0378 describes a sophisticated system in base FGDs. The “vehicles were deleted” shorthand is therefore too strong: the early accidental exposure was removed, but the system continued to evolve.

5.4 Gameplay, weapons and world structure

HEV suit and gravity gun. HEV sound effects (legacy pickup, plug detachment, a 1–9 container test) are in the engine; Tyler expects the suit to appear. Gravity-gun-related FGD entries were updated in 2021 and removed later; Tyler treats the gravity gun as likely but not confirmed as a usable weapon. The “Burbank suits” rename in #1 shows Valve actively scrubbing Half-Life names.

Weapons. Weapons were rewritten around VData. Flags support exhaustible/one-use weapons and weapons whose ammo never refills. Sound events cover primary, secondary, melee and special actions. HUD bucket positions follow a Half-Life-style weapon selector. Tyler explicitly says the one-use flag does not mean all HLX weapons are single-use.

World structure. Tyler rejects open world on technical grounds: Hammer’s 16,384-unit walk limit, baked lightmaps as the core lighting model, no traditional global illumination, and the scale of Deadlock/Neon Prime lighting. His description is “open linear,” comparable to Doom 2016’s Foundry or open-ended Uncharted 4 levels. Procedural spawn volumes and a director-style system may add variation inside those spaces, echoing the cancelled 2013 attempt.

Traversal and interaction. Physics-object pickup matches Half-Life 2; gravity shifting, vehicles, crouch/crawl states, level streaming, breakable props, and dynamic prop pickup/drop by NPCs round out the movement/interaction picture.

Multiplayer. Tyler understands HLX as single-player “as far as we understand.” The co-op question is unresolved; the corpus contains no verified multiplayer evidence.

5.5 NPC AI and combat

Navigation. NPC nav spaces/grids, moving platforms, gravity-shifted spaces, crawling, expanded movement capabilities, nav-link variants that can reference AG2 graphs, and AI level-of-detail.

Perception and mood. Mood files and a mood system; expanded sight-and-sound reactions; “AI volumetric events” replacing the older AI sound entity; last-known versus last-seen enemy tracking; deliberate misses; difficulty-scaled damage from all sources.

Damage and body parts. Four additional flinch/damage types: burn, sonic primary, sonic secondary and Newton. Destructible body parts generalize Alyx’s limited limb destruction into a full weak-point/dismemberment system, with hit-group damage and radius damage for parts. Tyler does not know which weapons use the new damage types.

Abilities, schedules and squads. A base NPC VData schema, abilities with proximity/range/cooldown/priority constraints, ability sound maps, NPC schedules, a rewritten squad system with squad slots, ally health regeneration, dynamic throwing/catching, and dropping/picking up/destroying abilities. Tyler compares the schedule ambition to Red Dead Redemption 2 but cannot confirm the task contents.

Tactical behaviours. Designer-placed Combine Charger shield deployments (absent in Alyx), dynamic sniper positions, signal/fallback hint nodes, NPC-run holdouts, dynamic physics-prop pickup/drop-off, a “flyer” flanking AI, a “threshold” tactical tip, and the “HLX cover preview” system with distance, peek, narrowness and model-size parameters. The cover system is a modernization of Half-Life 2’s node-based cover.

Caveat. Most of this is schema and tooling. It shows what the engine can express, not what shipped encounters use.

5.7 Story and Valve’s public statements

The post-Alyx setup. The Final Hours documents the January 2019 decision to have the G-Man save Eli at the end of Episode Two. Alyx’s post-credits scene shows Eli alive, Alyx gone, and Gordon receiving the crowbar. Sean Vanaman and Erik Wolpaw describe it as a deliberate promise of more. That is verified scene content. What happens next is unknown.

What Valve said, 2020–2024:

  • Robin Walker (March 2020): a Half-Life 3 team would have to answer “what is the big technological leap,” and “I don’t think there’s going to be another 13 years until the next one.”
  • Erik Wolpaw (March 2020): “I would love to do more,” and later “I’m ready to sign up for the next one.”
  • Gabe Newell (October 2020): the Alyx team is “incredibly fired up.”
  • Gabe Newell (July 2021): the Alyx ending is “the right progression.”
  • Greg Coomer (March 2022): “There are multiple games in development right now at Valve.”
  • Greg Coomer (August 2022): “We love that world and we want to continue to explore the Half-Life universe.”
  • Mike Morasky (September 2024): a recent project he “can’t talk about.”
  • Noclip’s 2024 Half-Life 2 documentary: Gabe Newell frames Half-Life as a vehicle for innovation; Brian Jacobson discusses the team’s willingness to rebuild.

These quotes establish intent and a live Half-Life universe. They do not name HLX, confirm a sequel, or date it. Morasky’s project is unidentified.

What is not established: whether HLX continues directly from the Alyx ending, whether Alyx is rescued, Gordon’s role, the G-Man’s goal, or any chapter structure. Fan scenarios in the corpus — an adult Alyx and Gordon’s child, or an arctic Borealis search — are speculation and should not be repeated as leaks.

Barney Calhoun. Tyler’s only recurring story claim is that Barney is “likely a part of it” (#9, 2025-02-26), stated while recapping what HLX “appears” to be; he cites no model, voice line, script, or casting evidence. In #8 (2025-01-31) he closed with a rhetorical aside that the player would “probably be walking around part of Half-Life 3 with Barney Calhoun as your sidekick because Alyx Vance is captured,” offered with no supporting detail and never repeated. Treat the first as low-confidence speculation and the second as an unelaborated aside, not a leak. No Barney-named model, VO file, or script is reported anywhere in the HLX material summarized here, but that is weak negative evidence only: Valve scrubbed HEV suit strings in the same period, so missing names prove little.

9. Method, evidence and limitations

Source archive

The archive layer records Wayback receipts for all 31 videos (all verified) and the Miro board (capture blocked by Miro, HTTP 403; the board export is the durable copy); source-manifest.tsv classifies all 610 events into build evidence, press, official statements, résumé/casting, community posts and inference. Most of the corpus is build evidence, which a web archive cannot preserve — those rows are marked as requiring a build snapshot rather than given an invented URL. Status: sources/archive-status.md; policy: sources/README.md.

Method notes

  • Dates: upload dates come from YouTube metadata, not playlist order. In-video dates are kept as stated and labeled.
  • Transcripts: auto-generated captions; names and technical terms are sometimes garbled. Summaries preserve Tyler’s hedging rather than silently resolving it.
  • Summaries: each video has one summary, written to a fixed schema. Structural consistency is checked mechanically; truth is not.
  • Fact vs. verified: Fact means a named artifact/official statement supports the claim; verified means the artifact is directly checkable. They overlap but are not identical.
  • Miro: the board is treated as a secondary research aid. Its dated notes are useful but not authoritative; where its wording conflicts with its own dates, the dates and build references win.
  • No source video was re-edited or re-uploaded. The VTT files are the immutable source of record.
  • Privacy: no individual actor or employee is identified; résumé claims are summarized without names or links.

Known limitations

  1. No official confirmation of HLX = Half-Life 3.
  2. No official release date; every timing statement is prediction or hearsay.
  3. Most engine features are shared Source 2 capability, not proof of HLX use.
  4. Auto-captions produce name/term errors; where possible the summary notes the transcription uncertainty.
  5. Miro metadata dates can be misleading.
  6. The 31-video set is incomplete: HLX Files #3/#13 and Every Last Detail Ep3 are absent.
  7. Verification is uneven: 400 events still need follow-up, and “Fact” labels were not independently re-checked.

10. Bottom line

The corpus supports a strong, consistent picture of a large Half-Life project in Source 2, publicly visible under the codename HLX since October 2020, with Half-Life-specific weapons, HEV suit, Xen fauna, NPC AI, vehicles, gravity shifting, liquid/fire simulation, Animgraph 2, and a deep Hammer/tooling stack.

It does not support any of the following as fact:

  • that Valve has officially called it Half-Life 3;
  • that it has a release date or a confirmed 2026 announcement;
  • that it is open world, VR, voxel-based, or built around a day/night cycle;
  • that “Project White Sands” is HLX;
  • that any specific enemy, location, or story beat is confirmed.

The most defensible summary is: HLX is a real, long-running, Half-Life-shaped Valve project that entered public datamining view in 2020, was still in intensive development in 2026, and has never been officially announced. Everything beyond that is Tyler’s inference, secondhand reporting, or a prediction — useful, but labeled as such.
