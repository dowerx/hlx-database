Overview

The video opens by correcting a widely shared story: a Dota 2 patch variable that leaked the “Dark Carnival” update name was renamed by Valve to “Why are you reading our variables?”, which Tyler says was a Dota 2-specific joke and not a message to Half-Life dataminers. The release of the Dark Carnival update then supplies the bulk of the video: a long list of strings in the HLX branch covering rendering, GPU-driven effects and audio, water and fluid behavior, animation, physics, and editor tooling. Tyler reads those strings as evidence that Valve raised the graphical and mechanical bar for HLX, and he connects that to the Steam Machine’s launch performance, Deadlock’s slower update cadence, and a Box3D blog post that names Ragnarok as a Rubicon evolution for future Valve games. The release-date and project-status material — the 2025 October/November expectations, the delay, and the claim that HLX occupies an entire floor — is presented explicitly as speculation from unnamed sources, with repeated reminders that Valve has said nothing officially.

  • Description: Tyler states that Ragnarok is not a new physics engine but an evolution, refactor, and optimization of Rubicon, the physics engine running in Source 2 since 2015.
