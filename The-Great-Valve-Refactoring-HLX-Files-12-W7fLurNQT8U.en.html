Overview

Tyler opens by pushing back on community panic over a roughly two-month lull in datamine news, restating that HLX remains in development and that the release schedule is unknown. The substantive portion concerns a large Counter-Strike 2 backend update that replaced the animation system and rewrote AI scripts, FGD definitions, and localization tokens, exposing HLX-specific entities, AI event types, and Hammer-facing documentation. He reads the new plain-language tooltips and surface-property descriptions as evidence that Valve is polishing its toolset for public use, and argues this resembles the end of development — while explicitly qualifying that this is Valve, not another studio. Confidence is mixed: the file strings and CS2 update are presented as primary datamined evidence, while the “end of development” reading is Tyler’s inference.
