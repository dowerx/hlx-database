Overview

This installment collects roughly six weeks of Tyler’s datamining findings rather than presenting a single new leak. Its centerpiece is the reappearance of surface attributes in a recent Deadlock update, now carrying material properties such as flash points and heat conductivity, which Tyler links back to a 2015 account of a “post-human” Gordon Freeman with an Aperture Science robotic arm from a purported 2013–2015 Half-Life 3 iteration. He also recaps a 2013 project description quoted from The Final Hours of Half-Life: Alyx, describes a 2021–2023 hiring wave drawn from major studios, and quotes two LinkedIn profiles about unannounced-project work. Confidence is mixed: the Deadlock file observations and quoted profile text are presented as evidence, while the 2015 conversation, the arm’s connection to current systems, and David Speyrer’s ongoing involvement are Tyler’s inference. He states plainly that this is a periodic catch-up video and that his research time is reduced while he moves house.

Surface attributes return in a Deadlock update with flash points and heat conductivity

  • Description: Tyler reports that a recent Deadlock update brought back surface attributes, long-standing Source engine data that gives geometry different behavior depending on what it simulates — concrete, metal, or wood — and how it reacts to player input. Surfaces now carry new behavior including their “flash point” (how hot a material must be to ignite) and heat conductivity (how quickly a material absorbs heat from nearby heat sources), and these values allow material destructibility to change based on how the player interacts with them.
  • Confidence: Fact — presented as a direct observation of the Deadlock update’s data, with the terminology (flash point, heat conductivity, fuel attachment) stated concretely.
  • Category: Engine feature
  • Source: Datamine of a recent Deadlock update; no build number or file path is given in the video.

  • Confidence: Mixed — the fuel-driven attribute modification is described as datamined behavior in Deadlock, while the claim that this is the successor to the 2015 arm concept is Tyler’s inference.
