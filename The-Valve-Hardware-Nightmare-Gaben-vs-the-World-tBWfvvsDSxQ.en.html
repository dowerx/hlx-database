Overview

Tyler argues that Valve’s 2026 hardware launch (Steam Machine, Steam Frame, Steam Controller) has slipped from “early 2026” promised pre-orders to a vague “2026” because of memory and storage shortages driven by AI data-center demand, on top of earlier tariff concerns. He traces the public messaging across the February 3 AMD earnings call, Valve’s February 4 FAQ post, and the edited March 6 Steam Year in Review post, then covers the GDC 2026 hardware talk: a semi-joking request for RAM, a reaffirmation that all three products ship in 2026, Steam Frame pricing “slightly less” than a $1,000 Index, and new verification requirements. The video is mostly a documented recap of public statements plus hands-on impressions relayed from GDC; Tyler’s own forward-looking price and Half-Life 3 claims are explicitly labeled as guesses.

Component shortages: US tariffs, then AI data-center demand for RAM

  • Description: Tyler attributes the stalled pre-orders first to international tariffs placed on goods within the United States that could have affected long-term component sourcing, and then to AI data centers consuming the RAM supply (“literally all of it”) plus rising SSD prices that have a knock-on effect across the industry. Pre-order information never arrived, and the silence became “palpable” around February 2026.
  • Confidence: Speculation — Tyler’s causal framing (“it became clear that Valve had hit another road block,” “a lot of people, including myself, started speculating that the hardware had hit an internal delay”) with no document cited for the tariff claim.
  • Category: Business/legal
  • Source: Tyler’s analysis of industry reporting on tariffs, memory, and storage pricing.
  • Earlier mentions: None identified in this video.
  • Verification: needs follow-up — Valve’s February 4 FAQ confirms memory and storage shortages as the stated cause; the tariff-sourcing link is not confirmed by Valve.
